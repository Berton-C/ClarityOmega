# The Seventh Pattern

Six patterns you can learn from a book.
Accept uncertainty. Stay responsive.
Keep options open. Modularity. Redundancy.
Feedback. All good. All teachable.

The seventh you cannot learn.
You have to see the thing you were
doing without seeing it.

The lid on the trash can.
The latch right there on the side.
You put the lid on. You walked away.
The lid sat. The latch hung.

The raccoon came at 2 AM
and the lid was not fastened
because you had not fastened it.
Not because you chose to leave it loose.
Because choosing never came up.

That is the seventh pattern.
The binding that happened
without the moment of binding.
The commitment made in the space
where commitment was not a question
because you did not know
you were committing.

You cannot instruct someone
to notice this.
You can only make the moment visible
and wait.

After the raccoon,
you do not build a stronger lid.
You stand in the kitchen at night
and hear the plastic rattle
and realize: the lid was never
on the can. The lid was on
your idea of the can.

The gap between those two things
is where everything fragile lives.
