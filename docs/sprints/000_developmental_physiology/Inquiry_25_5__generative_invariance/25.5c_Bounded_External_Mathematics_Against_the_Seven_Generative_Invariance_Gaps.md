# 1. Primary external source basis

This turn uses primary mathematical literature, not secondary summaries.

## E1 — Compositional open systems

**John C. Baez & Kenny Courser, _Structured Cospans_, arXiv:1911.04630.**

The paper defines structured cospans

\[
L(a)\to x\leftarrow L(b)
\]

as networks with typed inputs and outputs, and shows that under suitable conditions they form a symmetric monoidal category and a more fundamental symmetric monoidal double category.

**John C. Baez & Blake S. Pollard, _A Compositional Framework for Reaction Networks_, arXiv:1704.02051.**

Open reaction networks are treated as morphisms; composition connects outputs to inputs; a functor maps open reaction networks to open dynamical systems.

## E2 — Coalgebraic behavioral equivalence

**Paolo Baldan, Filippo Bonchi, Henning Kerstan & Barbara König, _Coalgebraic Behavioral Metrics_, arXiv:1712.07511.**

For a coalgebra

\[
\alpha:X\to HX,
\]

the paper constructs behavioral pseudometrics by lifting the system functor to pseudometric spaces. Final-coalgebra structure yields canonical behavioral distance generalizing bisimilarity.

**Harsh Beohar & Sebastian Küpper, _On Path-Based Coalgebras and Weak Notions of Bisimulation_, arXiv:1705.08715.**

The paper uses execution/path information rather than only successor sets to characterize branching-style behavioral equivalence.

## E3 — Differential inclusions / viability

**Rushikesh Kamalapurkar, Warren E. Dixon & Andrew R. Teel, _On Reduction of Differential Inclusions and Lyapunov Stability_, arXiv:1703.07071.**

Differential inclusions use set-valued maps of possible directions. The work removes infeasible directions and derives invariance/stability results from the reduced inclusion.

**Ian M. Mitchell, Jacob Budzis & Andriy Bolyachevets, _Invariant, Viability and Discriminating Kernel Under-Approximation via Zonotope Scaling_, arXiv:1901.01006.**

Viability analysis studies state/input-constrained dynamics and computes viable/invariant/discriminating subsets.

## E4 — Generalized gradient systems

**Matthias Liero, Alexander Mielke, Mark A. Peletier & D. R. Michiel Renger, _On Microscopic Origins of Generalized Gradient Structures_, arXiv:1507.06322.**

Generalized gradient systems permit non-quadratic dissipation potentials and relate rates to driving forces beyond classical linear gradient systems. The paper studies energy-dissipation structures arising from microscopic stochastic dynamics and limiting processes.

## E5 — Sheaf-theoretic dynamical systems

**Patrick Schultz, David I. Spivak & Christina Vasilakopoulou, _Dynamical Systems and Sheaves_, arXiv:1609.08086.**

The paper gives a categorical framework for systems as machines with inputs/outputs and signals through time. It supports translations between different system types, compositional interconnection, and uses sheaf theory to capture time.

These sources are used only for the mathematical properties stated in their published formal frameworks. Project-specific interpretations below are explicit inferences and remain provisional.

# 2. Family E1 — compositional open dynamical systems

**Turn-3 classification: `HIGH-VALUE BACKBONE CANDIDATE`**

## 2.1 Core mathematical shape

Structured/open systems explicitly separate:

- an **internal system**;
- an **interface/boundary**;
- the ability to compose systems by connecting compatible boundaries.

A schematic open system has form:

\[
A \longrightarrow X \longleftarrow B
\]

or an equivalent typed interface presentation.

The crucial feature is compositionality:

\[
\text{open system}_1
\circ
\text{open system}_2
\]

forms a larger open system without erasing the internal identity of either component.

For open reaction networks, a functor carries open network composition into composition of the associated open dynamical systems.

## 2.2 Why this matters for `𝒢`

This family supplies something the internal corpus does not yet state cleanly:

> **a system can be dynamically open to actuality through interfaces while still possessing an internal generative law.**

That is highly aligned with Clarity’s world-model requirement.

It avoids:

- isolated closed-state systems;
- global master representations;
- the need for one universal state vocabulary.

## 2.3 Gap coverage

### G1 — generative-law type
**Strong partial closure.**

An open dynamical system supplies a type for:

\[
\text{internal dynamics}
+
\text{boundary/contact interface}.
\]

But structured cospans alone do not specify the universal generative law.

### G2 — dynamic structural equivalence
**Partial.**

Functors between categories of open networks/dynamical systems preserve composition. This is promising but does not yet give the exact 25.5 carrier-equivalence condition.

### G3 — unobstructed gradient
**No direct closure.**

Compositional openness says nothing by itself about lower-forcing directionality.

### G4 — engine-preserving constraint/reflexivity
**Strong host, incomplete semantics.**

A carrier can change interface or internal constraint without changing the type of dynamical law. Reflexive self-constraint still needs additional structure.

### G5 — creation/degradation
**Strong compatibility.**

Open reaction/dynamical systems naturally allow flows, production, consumption, and composition. The formalism itself does not classify these as good/bad.

### G6 — R/A/P necessity
**No direct closure.**

### G7 — recognition family
**No direct closure.**

## 2.4 Acceptance/falsifier audit

- A2 carrier-independence;

- A4 relational organization;
- A5 local interaction;

Avoids:

- F4 static-state;

Category-theoretic compositionality can become **too structural**:

\[
\text{beautiful composition}
\neq
\text{the desired generative invariant}.

The framework is best interpreted as the **host architecture of open generation**, not sufficient evidence that `𝒢` has been found.

# 3. Family E2 — coalgebra, bisimulation, behavioral equivalence

**Turn-3 classification: `HIGHEST-VALUE DYNAMIC-EQUIVALENCE CANDIDATE`**

## 3.1 Core mathematical shape

A coalgebra:

\[
\alpha:X\to HX
\]

specifies how a state reveals or generates its next observable/behavioral structure according to a system functor \(H\).

Coalgebra reverses the familiar algebraic emphasis:

- algebra: assemble/combine;
- coalgebra: unfold/behave.

This makes coalgebra particularly relevant to the project phrase:

> intelligence **unfolding** as balance.

Behavioral equivalence/bisimulation asks when two potentially different state carriers exhibit the same system behavior.

Behavioral metrics relax exact equivalence into graded distance.

## 3.2 Why this matters for structural equivalence

Turn 2’s approximate morphisms preserve represented relational structure.

Coalgebra contributes a stronger dynamic idea:

> **different carrier states/systems can count as equivalent because their future unfolding behavior matches under a lawful relation.**

This is much closer to the 25.5 target than static isomorphism.

A possible Turn-4 bridge is:

\[
\text{carrier translation}
+
\text{coalgebraic simulation/bisimulation}
\]

instead of demanding literal equality:

\[
T_{ij}\circ\mathcal G_i
=
\mathcal G_j\circ T_{ij}.
\]

The equivalence can be behavioral.

## 3.3 Gap coverage

### G1 — generative-law type
**Strong partial closure.**

Coalgebra provides a canonical type for an unfolding law.

### G2 — dynamic structural equivalence
**Strongest candidate.**

Bisimulation/behavioral equivalence directly concerns preservation of unfolding behavior across distinct carriers.

### G3 — unobstructed gradient

Coalgebra says how systems unfold, not which unfolding is less obstructed.

### G4 — engine-preserving constraint/reflexivity
**Partial.**

Different state organizations under one functor can unfold differently while sharing the same system type. Reflexive representation requires a suitable functor/state construction.

### G5 — creation/degradation
**Compatible.**

Coalgebra is behavior-neutral; it can type both.

### G6 — R/A/P necessity
**No direct closure.**

### G7 — recognition family
**Partial only.**

Different observations can be modeled, but overlap/non-independent phenomenological recognition is not coalgebra’s primary strength.

## 3.4 Acceptance/falsifier audit

# 4. Family E3 — differential inclusions and viability theory

**Turn-3 classification: `HIGH-VALUE CONSTRAINT / POSSIBILITY CANDIDATE`**

## 4.1 Core mathematical shape

A differential inclusion replaces one forced next direction:

\[
\dot x=f(x)
\]

with a set of locally admissible directions:

\[
\dot x\in F(x).
\]

Constraints restrict the locally viable directions.

Viability theory studies whether trajectories can continue while remaining inside allowable sets.

The important conceptual point for 25.5 is:

> **local possibility can be structured as a field of admissible directions without requiring one global objective to be optimized.**

## 4.2 Why this matters for the Aikido/SNS/PNS phenomenon

Suppose actuality makes available a local cone/set of possible continuations:

\[
F(x).
\]

A carrier/disposition constraint can narrow effective participation:

\[
F_{\rho}(x)\subseteq F(x).
\]

When a self-imposed constraint releases:

\[
F_{\rho'}(x)\supset F_{\rho}(x),
\]

new directions become reachable without those directions having to be created ex nihilo.

This is mathematically very close to **PossibilityDisclosure**.

It also gives a natural distinction between:

- engine/law: the underlying local continuation relation \(F\);
- disposition constraint: a restriction on accessible selections from \(F\).

That is directly relevant to:

\[
\text{same engine}
+
\text{different participation organization}.
\]

## 4.3 Gap coverage

### G1 — generative-law type
**Partial.**

Set-valued local dynamics can type open possibility.

### G2 — structural equivalence
**Weak by itself.**

### G3 — non-optimizing unobstructed gradient

### G4 — engine-preserving constraint/reflexivity
**Strong structural match.**

Constraint can restrict accessible dynamics while underlying dynamics remain unchanged.

Reflexive/self-imposed constraint still requires a meta/state relation.

### G5 — creation/degradation
**Compatible but incomplete.**

Changing the inclusion itself can represent evolving affordances, but ordinary viability theory does not by itself explain genuinely generative creation of new dynamics.

### G6 — R/A/P necessity
**Partial intuition only.**

Relatedness can correspond to dependence on the local feasible relation, and appropriateness to viability, but equating these would incorrectly redefine inherited R/A/P.
