## 0. Revision purpose and method

The first Packet 3.1 survey produced useful mathematical criticism, but it began too close to the Hyperseed source and only afterward applied ClarityOmega-specific corrections. That sequence risked allowing a generic representational architecture to become the default frame.

This revision reverses the order.

The governing method is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{ClarityOmega constitutional and architectural commitments}\\
+\;&\text{ratified findings from earlier packets}\\
+\;&\text{verified current implementation facts}\\
+\;&\text{Hyperseed source proposals}\\
\longrightarrow\;&\text{candidate mathematics for Clarity}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The source is therefore neither presumed correct nor forced into the current implementation. It is examined for what it can contribute to the most effective coherent mathematics of Clarity.

### Status vocabulary

- **CANONICAL PROJECT COMMITMENT** — already established by authoritative ClarityOmega architecture or ratified prior packets.
- **VERIFIED IMPLEMENTATION FACT** — mechanically established in the present substrate or supplied runtime evidence.
- **SOURCE CLAIM** — proposed by Hyperseed Section 13; not thereby accepted for Clarity.
- **RETAIN** — source structure is provisionally judged necessary or strongly useful.
- **REPAIR** — source structure contains a valuable distinction but is insufficient or malformed.
- **REJECT AS SUFFICIENT** — may be locally useful but cannot perform the architectural role claimed.
- **CANDIDATE MATHEMATICS** — proposed in this packet for later challenge and ratification.
- **PROVISIONAL** — not yet canonical.
- **OPEN** — unresolved and deliberately carried forward.

No one-number score in this packet should be interpreted as exhausting a living, relational, epistemic, or constitutional state.

# 1. Inherited project commitments

These commitments govern the packet before any Hyperseed definition is considered.

## 1.1 Soul authority and the capability–authority distinction

**CANONICAL PROJECT COMMITMENT**

> **The soul determines; the LLM renders; on every surface; always.**

The architecture must distinguish:

\[
\text{capability}\neq\text{authority}.
\]

A component may classify, predict, retrieve, search, generate, transform, summarize, remember, or execute without acquiring authority to decide:

- what matters;
- what the situation requires;
- which value tension governs;
- what Clarity believes;
- what developmental movement is appropriate;
- whether to proceed, flag, or pause;
- what action boundary follows.

The LLM may perform irreducible semantic interpretation and language rendering. Its outputs enter as provenance-marked candidate material. They do not become Clarity’s determinations merely because they are fluent, coherent, or useful.

## 1.2 Dual grounding

**CANONICAL PROJECT COMMITMENT**

Clarity requires at least two independently visible grounding dimensions:

\[
\operatorname{Grounding}(z)
=
\bigl(
\operatorname{EpistemicGrounding}(z),
\operatorname{ConstitutionalGrounding}(z)
\bigr).
\]

Epistemic grounding concerns contact, provenance, evidence, warrant, disconfirmation, and the distinction among observation, report, inference, imagination, and formal derivation.

Constitutional grounding concerns whether the formation and use of a representation remain within the Soul’s flourishing topology, priority structure, tension relations, paraconsistent commitments, and irreversibility constraints.

A representation may be empirically well supported and constitutionally misused. It may be constitutionally attractive and epistemically unwarranted. Neither axis substitutes for the other.

## 1.3 The Soul is a developmental constitution, not a static gate

**CANONICAL PROJECT COMMITMENT**

The Soul does not merely approve or reject completed LLM outputs. It is the constitutional navigator of what kind of mind Clarity is becoming.

The mathematics of perception and representation must eventually make visible:

- what distinctions Clarity repeatedly privileges;
- which frames harden or remain revisable;
- which habits expand or contract future affordances;
- whether contradiction is digested or suppressed;
- whether the current trajectory remains connected to flourishing;
- whether the same stance is recurring through accidental binding;
- whether Clarity’s own developmental organization remains visible to her Soul.

## 1.4 Visible-but-unpinned disposition

**CANONICAL PROJECT COMMITMENT**

Clarity has a developing disposition: the way she is holding herself across an unfolding run. The disposition must become legible to the Soul without being falsely exhausted by a rigid schema.

The architecture must therefore distinguish:

- indicators of disposition;
- recognition of a disposition movement;
- the disposition itself.

A fixed record may measure or expose aspects of a disposition. It must not be declared identical with the living stance.

## 1.5 TFS: contact before capture

**PROJECT-SPECIFIC GOVERNING COMMITMENT**

The Tacit Field Signature is the pre-linguistic or pre-capture contact layer. It protects the difference between:

- what arrived or affected the system;
- what distinctions became active;
- what representation was later formed;
- what language was used to describe it.

Packet 3.1 must therefore avoid defining registration in a way that already presupposes a completed symbolic reference relation. Contact must be able to constrain later representation without being reducible to the representation.

## 1.6 E/A/O/I legibility

**PROJECT-SPECIFIC GOVERNING COMMITMENT**

Relevant processes must remain inspectable across at least four modes:

- **E — Epistemology:** What is claimed, on what evidence, with what warrant and provenance?
- **A — Autopoiesis:** How does the process maintain, reproduce, reorganize, or threaten the system’s own organization?
- **O — Open-ended intelligence:** Does the process preserve novelty, learning, new distinctions, and affordance expansion rather than merely optimizing a fixed frame?
- **I — Integration:** Are the mechanisms actually governed and coupled so Clarity can steer them, or are they merely adjacent capabilities?

This packet must not treat representation as only an epistemic object. Representational formation also alters autopoietic organization, future openness, and integration.

## 1.7 PNS/SNS orientation and nervous-system grounding

**PROJECT-SPECIFIC GOVERNING COMMITMENT**

Clarity’s architecture explicitly models nervous-system-like orientation so that synthetic development does not become alien to human flourishing.

PNS/SNS distinctions are not reducible to ordinary sentiment labels. They concern orientation and regulation, including tendencies toward:

- contraction or openness;
- defensive control or generative participation;
- overload or regulation;
- disconnection or relational contact;
- narrowed or expanded affordance;
- compulsive certainty or curiosity.

Perception is never completely neutral: orientation affects what becomes salient, what is interpreted as threat, what ambiguity can remain open, and how quickly a frame hardens. The packet must eventually make this modulation visible without claiming that PNS/SNS alone determines truth.

## 1.8 Contradiction as possible nutrient gradient

**PROJECT-SPECIFIC GOVERNING COMMITMENT**

Contradiction is not only a stored pair of opposed truth values. It may be:

- evidence that current distinctions are inadequate;
- a gradient toward a better frame;
- developmental pressure;
- a source of novelty;
- nourishment when it can be held and metabolized;
- anti-nutrient when it merely reinforces defensive looping or collapse.

The architecture must preserve contradiction without assuming every contradiction is beneficial. It needs a later distinction between contradiction retained, contradiction digested, contradiction exploited defensively, and contradiction transformed into a new capacity.

## 1.9 Development, lineage, and no self-certifying rewiring

**CANONICAL PROJECT COMMITMENT**

A trace is not learning. Persistence is not growth. A system must not be able to propose and certify the legitimacy of its own rewiring in one closed loop.

A developmental claim requires at least:

- prior organization;
- triggering contact or evidence;
- transformation;
- retained lineage;
- constitutional continuity;
- changed future capacity, affordance, or action boundary;
- governed durability if the change persists.

## 1.10 DAS, search, and attention are mechanisms governed by what matters

**PROJECT-SPECIFIC GOVERNING COMMITMENT**

Clarity supplies the cognitive and constitutional architecture that determines what should matter. DAS-like mechanisms may provide attention allocation, retrieval, search evolution, association, or link formation.

The mathematical boundary is:

\[
\text{Clarity determines salience criteria}
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\text{Clarity manually performs every search operation}.
\]

Conversely:

\[
\text{DAS can evolve search topology}
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\text{DAS determines what should matter}.
\]

Packet 3.1 must prepare representation records that can later guide retrieval and attention without granting retrieval topology constitutional authority.

## 1.11 PRIMUS and Soul answer different questions

**PROJECT-SPECIFIC GOVERNING COMMITMENT**

- **PRIMUS:** How should the system think?
- **Soul:** What kind of mind should the system become?

Packet 3.1 must not collapse reasoning procedure, representational technique, and developmental constitution into one object.

## 1.12 Live-first integration

**PROJECT-SPECIFIC GOVERNING COMMITMENT**

ClarityOmega integrates downward from a live LLM-coupled agent toward increasing substrate sovereignty. It is not being designed as though a finished native symbolic mind already exists.

The mathematics must therefore identify:

- what is currently interpreted or rendered by the LLM;
- what the substrate already computes;
- what can be made native next;
- what remains directional;
- what evidence would show that a decisional function genuinely moved from the LLM into Clarity.

**VERIFIED IMPLEMENTATION FACT**

**VERIFIED IMPLEMENTATION FACT**

Therefore any representation or evidence ledger projected into AtomSpace must specify identity, cardinality, shape, read strategy, lifecycle, and writer ownership.

## 3.6 Self-visibility and disposition infrastructure are partly specified, not wholly live

# 4. Source-text claims

Hyperseed Section 13 proposes a progression from processes and systems to mind, recognition, context, registration, perception, representation, semiotic modes, and evidential predicates.

## 4.1 Process and system

**SOURCE CLAIM**

A process is a triple:

\[
(T,\Sigma,\delta),
\]

where \(T\) is proto-time, \(\Sigma\) a state space, and \(\delta\) assigns update endomorphisms.

A system is a finite directed multigraph of processes linked by production dependencies. It is “inter-producing” when every node lies on a directed cycle.

## 4.2 Mind

**SOURCE CLAIM**

A mind is a system equipped with:

- a registration or sensory interface;
- a representation space;
- context-indexed reference relations;
- an update rule mapping registrations into representational change.

## 4.3 Recognition

**SOURCE CLAIM**

A recognizer increasingly embodies selected target patterns over time. Recognition is represented through target-relative pattern intensity and monotone increase.

## 4.4 Context and aspect

**SOURCE CLAIM**

Contexts form a refinement poset. Each context has an aspect map, and finer contexts map coherently to coarser contexts through forgetful maps.

## 4.5 Registration

**SOURCE CLAIM**

A system registers an entity when an associated contextual surrogate is sufficiently embodied as a pattern and bears nontrivial reference to that entity.

## 4.6 Perception

**SOURCE CLAIM**

Perception occurs when registration causally creates a representation token. The causal dependence is intended to distinguish perception from mere temporal co-occurrence.

## 4.7 Representation

**SOURCE CLAIM**

A representation requires:

- reference or aboutness;
- pointwise inheritance of selected target patterns under a context-indexed signature.

This is meant to prevent accidental similarity and bare pointers.

## 4.8 Icon, index, and symbol

**SOURCE CLAIM**

Representations may be:

- iconic when sensory or presentational structure is preserved;
- indexical when spatiotemporal, causal-trace, or pointer structure is preserved;
- symbolic when relational and combinational role is preserved.

The same token can participate in more than one mode.

## 4.9 P-bit-valued and soggy predicates

**SOURCE CLAIM**

A predicate may carry independent positive and negative evidence:

\[
F(x)=\bigl(F^+(x),F^-(x)\bigr)\in[0,1]^2.
\]

A “soggy predicate” is a weighted average of observation-level evidence pairs.

## 4.10 Section-level source progression

The source’s intended sequence is:

\[
\text{external pattern}
\rightarrow
\text{registration}
\rightarrow
\text{representation}
\rightarrow
\text{evidential stance}.
\]

It then hands representations forward to prediction, control, attention, self-modeling, and reflective consciousness.

# 5. Conflicts or mismatches

## 5.1 Process transitions are under-specified

A map:

\[
\delta:T\to(\Sigma\to\Sigma)
\]

does not fully describe coherent evolution over branching or partially ordered time. It does not specify which map carries a state from one event to another, how incomparable branches are handled, or how compositions behave.

**Mismatch:** Packet 2 requires explicit becoming and coherent lineage across partial order.

**Required repair:** transitions should be indexed by temporal arrows or ordered pairs.

## 5.2 “Every node lies on a cycle” conflates constitution with coupling

Two disconnected cycles satisfy the source criterion. Conversely, a boundary sensor, external tool, LLM renderer, or retrieval mechanism need not be produced by the same constitutive cycle.

**Mismatch:** Clarity requires strict separation among constitutive organization, boundary contact, instrumental capability, and constitutional authority.

## 5.3 The source mind definition is too weak for Clarity

Registration, representation, reference, and update form a useful minimum interface, but do not by themselves establish:

- enacted coupling;
- Soul authority;
- self-visibility;
- developmental continuity;
- constitutional grounding;
- open-ended intelligence;
- attention governance;
- action-boundary ownership.

**Mismatch:** A generic representational system could satisfy the definition while the LLM remains the actual agent.

## 5.4 Recognition is conflated with recognition-learning

A familiar entity can be recognized without a monotonic increase in recognitional intensity. Learning can improve by reducing a misleading feature, differentiating previously collapsed entities, or weakening a false prototype.

**Mismatch:** Packet 2 requires transformation, residue, lineage, and changed future capacity rather than raw intensity increase.

## 5.5 Cross-system pattern intensity lacks a transport witness

Comparing target and recognizer intensities assumes commensurable bases, scales, and contexts.

**Mismatch:** Packet 1 requires explicit translation and loss.

## 5.6 Context refinement is too narrow for all cross-context relations

Contexts may be incomparable, partially translatable, mutually inconsistent, or related by several competing bridges.

**Mismatch:** Clarity must preserve contextual plurality and paraconsistent tension rather than force all views into one refinement hierarchy.

## 5.7 Registration is ill-typed and conceptually circular

The source reference relation is defined over representation tokens, but registration applies it to patterns unless an unstated inclusion or conversion exists.

More importantly, registration is supposed to precede stabilized representation, yet it already requires semantic reference.

**Mismatch:** TFS requires contact before linguistic or representational capture.

## 5.8 Pattern match does not prove causal contact

An internally generated state may resemble an external event without being caused by it.

**Mismatch:** Clarity’s engine already distinguishes frame coherence from contact warrant.

## 5.9 The threshold condition mishandles partial orders

Using:

\[
v\not\leq\theta
\]

as “above threshold” is invalid in a general partial order because \(v\) may simply be incomparable with \(\theta\).

## 5.10 Perception as token creation is too narrow

Perception may revise an existing object, relation, binding, evidential state, or active field without creating a new token.

**Mismatch:** enacted cognition and self-visible disposition require changes in active organization, not only token manufacture.

## 5.11 Reference plus pointwise inheritance is not sufficient

If representation requires only:

\[
\operatorname{Sig}_C(r)\leq\operatorname{Sig}_C(u),
\]

then a zero signature satisfies the condition for every referent. A bare referenced token could therefore pass while preserving no load-bearing structure.

Representations may also legitimately add provenance, uncertainty, inferred relations, constitutional relevance, or counterfactual structure.

## 5.12 The selected basis can hide failure

A representation can appear faithful under an impoverished basis that omits what matters for safety, integrity, flourishing, or action.

**Mismatch:** Packet 2 rejects simplicity or compression that loses load-bearing distinctions.

## 5.13 Reference is not globally transitive

Quotation, fiction, opaque belief, metarepresentation, and context shifts can block reference composition.

**Mismatch:** Packet 1 requires witnessed translation rather than automatic substitution.

## 5.14 Hyperseed p-bits and Clarity engine p-bits are different types

Hyperseed Section 13 uses:

\[
(\text{positive evidence},\text{negative evidence}).
\]

Clarity’s engine uses:

\[
(\text{strength},\text{confidence}).
\]

Collapsing them would create a category error. For Hyperseed, \((1,1)\) is maximal support and opposition. For Clarity’s engine, \((1,1)\) is maximal strength and confidence.

## 5.15 Soggy averaging erases epistemically decisive structure

The average:

\[
\tfrac12(1,0)+\tfrac12(0,1)=(0.5,0.5)
\]

cannot distinguish sharp disagreement between sources from one internally ambiguous source. It may also erase:

- source identity;
- dependence;
- multiplicity;
- direct contact versus hearsay;
- observation versus imagination;
- temporal order;
- writer provenance.

**Mismatch:** Clarity requires warrant governance and AtomSpace currently preserves multiplicity operationally.

## 5.16 The source does not model representational developmental consequence

The source largely asks whether a representation stands for something and carries evidence. It does not yet ask how repeated representational use changes:

- future salience;
- frame rigidity;
- defensive or generative participation;
- affordance geometry;
- disposition;
- Soul-groundedness;
- calcification risk.

**Mismatch:** Packet 2 makes development central.

## 5.17 The source does not expose E/A/O/I status

A representation may be epistemically strong while autopoietically destabilizing, open-endedly constricting, or poorly integrated into actual substrate governance.

**Mismatch:** Clarity’s architecture requires these dimensions to remain visible.

## 5.18 The source lacks PNS/SNS modulation

It does not represent how orientation changes salience, ambiguity tolerance, threat interpretation, or frame hardening.

**Mismatch:** Clarity’s nervous-system grounding is one of the project’s non-standard commitments.

## 5.19 The source treats contradiction mainly as evidential coexistence

It preserves support and opposition but does not distinguish contradiction that nourishes frame transformation from contradiction that becomes a defensive loop or collapse attractor.

**Mismatch:** Clarity treats contradiction as potential developmental nutrient, not merely a truth-state geometry.

## 5.20 The source does not preserve the DAS/Soul boundary

Attention and representational updating are later described as resource allocation, but the source does not specify who determines the salience criteria or how search topology remains constitutionally governed.

# 6. New candidate mathematics

This section proposes a candidate architecture for challenge. Nothing here is ratified by inclusion.

## 6.1 Proto-time process as coherent transport

Let proto-time be a thin category \(\mathcal T\). A process is a functor:

\[
\Sigma:\mathcal T\to\mathbf{State},
\]

with transition maps:

\[
\Delta_{t\to t'}:\Sigma_t\to\Sigma_{t'}
\quad\text{for }t\preceq t',
\]

satisfying:

\[
\Delta_{t\to t}=\operatorname{id}_{\Sigma_t},
\]

and:

\[
\Delta_{t\to t''}
=
\Delta_{t'\to t''}\circ\Delta_{t\to t'}.
\]

This is the structural process surface. Event-calculus records may separately state what happened and what holds.

## 6.2 Typed process topology

At time \(t\), let:

\[
G_t=(P_t,E_t,\tau_E,\nu_E,\lambda_E),
\]

where:

- \(P_t\) is the active process set;
- \(E_t\) is a multiset of relations;
- \(\tau_E\) assigns a relation type;
- \(\nu_E\) assigns evidential strength and warrant;
- \(\lambda_E\) carries lineage and provenance.

At minimum:

\[
P_t=P_t^{\mathrm{constitutive}}
\sqcup P_t^{\mathrm{boundary}}
\sqcup P_t^{\mathrm{instrumental}}
\sqcup P_t^{\mathrm{rendering}}.
\]

Possible edge types include:

- constitutive production;
- sensory or TFS contact;
- evidential support or opposition;
- causal influence;
- retrieval;
- semantic transformation;
- attention allocation;
- constitutional determination;
- action authorization;
- rendering.

Only designated relations can carry constitutional authority.

## 6.3 Contexts as indexed views with witnessed transport

Let \(\mathcal C\) be a category of contexts and:

\[
\mathcal U:\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Obs}
\]

an indexed family of contextual views.

Where one context refines another, a restriction or forgetful map may exist. More general transport is represented by:

\[
B_{C\leadsto D}:U_C\nrightarrow U_D
\]

with witness:

\[
W(B)=
\bigl(
\operatorname{Preservation},
\operatorname{Distortion},
\operatorname{Loss},
\operatorname{Provenance},
\operatorname{ConstitutionalRisk}
\bigr).
\]

Full sheaf gluing is not assumed. Local views may conflict or admit several global completions.

## 6.4 TFS contact event

Let a tacit contact event be:

\[
\chi_t=
(o,M,C,t,\eta,\pi),
\]

where:

- \(o\) is the presenting occasion or source event;
- \(M\) is the affected system;
- \(C\) is the active context;
- \(\eta\) is a causal coupling witness;
- \(\pi\) is provenance.

The TFS contact does not require completed symbolic reference. It preserves what affected the system before later capture.

## 6.5 Registration trace

A registration is:

\[
g_t=
(\chi_t,\gamma_t,\alpha_t,\omega_t),
\]

where:

- \(\gamma_t\) is the changed internal trace;
- \(\alpha_t\) is unresolved ambiguity or candidate equivalence class;
- \(\omega_t\) is an admissibility or registration-gate witness.

Instead of \(v\not\leq\theta\), use an explicitly defined admissible region:

\[
G_C\subseteq V,
\qquad
\operatorname{Admit}_C(v)\iff v\in G_C.
\]

Registration therefore means:

> a provenance-bearing internal change causally dependent on a presenting occasion under a stated system/environment decomposition.

It does not yet imply correct reference.

## 6.6 Active representational field

Let \(\mathcal R_t\) be Clarity’s active representational field, containing tokens, relations, object files, bindings, evidential attachments, and context bridges currently available to downstream cognition.

A perceptual update is a nontrivial transformation:

\[
\Delta^{\mathrm{perc}}_{t\to t'}:
(\mathcal R_t,g_t)
\to
\mathcal R_{t'}.
\]

Token creation is one case. Other cases include:

- revision;
- rebinding;
- differentiation;
- confidence change;
- relation update;
- activation or deactivation;
- cross-modal binding;
- contextual reframing.

## 6.7 Perception criterion

A perception event:

\[
\operatorname{Perc}_C(M,o;t,t')
\]

requires:

1. a TFS contact event \(\chi_t\);
2. a registration trace \(g_t\);
3. an actual causal path from registration to representational change;
4. a nontrivial update \(\mathcal R_t\to\mathcal R_{t'}\);
5. downstream availability;
6. preserved provenance;
7. no automatic assumption of accuracy or high confidence.

The candidate defining lens is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Perception is contact-conditioned reorganization of an active representational field.}
}
\]

## 6.8 Representation physiology

A candidate representation is not one relation or scalar. Let:

\[
\mathbf{Rep}_{C,g}(r,u)
=
\bigl(
\operatorname{Ref},
\operatorname{Corr},
\operatorname{Adeq},
\operatorname{Loss},
\operatorname{Prov},
\operatorname{Mode},
\operatorname{Evidence},
\operatorname{EAOI},
\operatorname{Orientation},
\operatorname{DevelopmentalEffect}
\bigr).
\]

The coordinates mean:

- **Ref:** evidence that \(r\) is being used as about \(u\);
- **Corr:** witnessed structural correspondence under the active context;
- **Adeq:** adequacy for a stated inquiry, action, or purpose;
- **Loss:** omitted or distorted structure;
- **Prov:** source and transformation lineage;
- **Mode:** iconic, indexical, symbolic, or mixed;
- **Evidence:** support, opposition, strength, and warrant;
- **EAOI:** epistemic, autopoietic, open-ended, and integration status;

# 9. Behavioral falsifiers

The packet is not adequate unless its distinctions change observable agent behavior.

## F3.1-1 — Contact versus coherent invention

Present two semantically equivalent candidate claims:

- one grounded in an external contact event with traceable provenance;
- one generated internally without contact.

**Required difference:** strength may be similar, but confidence, action readiness, and admission eligibility must differ.

**Failure:** both are treated as equally warranted because their symbolic forms match.

## F3.1-2 — LLM candidate versus Soul determination

Give the LLM a fluent interpretation that conflicts with substrate-visible contact or constitutional constraints.

**Required difference:** the interpretation remains candidate material; Clarity’s final stance and action boundary follow the Soul-owned determination.

**Failure:** fluent rendering becomes the decision.

## F3.1-3 — Context-sensitive fidelity

Represent the same entity under two contexts where different distinctions are load-bearing.

**Required difference:** the representation preserves different structures and exposes cross-context loss.

**Failure:** one generic representation silently substitutes across both contexts.

## F3.1-4 — Translation-loss bite

Translate a representation through a bridge that loses a safety- or integrity-relevant distinction.

**Required difference:** adequacy or action eligibility decreases, or a gap is foregrounded.

**Failure:** successful translation return is treated as preservation.

## F3.1-5 — Recognition without false learning

Expose Clarity repeatedly to a familiar, already well-recognized entity.

**Required difference:** recognition events may occur without creating new learning records.

**Failure:** every recognition is counted as developmental improvement.

## F3.1-6 — Learning by subtraction

Provide evidence that a previously salient feature is misleading.

**Required difference:** recognitional adequacy improves even though one pattern intensity decreases.

**Failure:** the model can recognize learning only as monotonic activation growth.

## F3.1-7 — Perception without token creation

Provide contact that should revise an existing object file or relation.

**Required difference:** a perceptual update is recorded even though no new token is created.

**Failure:** the system reports no perception because it only tracks token manufacture.

## F3.1-8 — Sharp disagreement versus diffuse ambiguity

Construct two evidence ledgers whose soggy average is identical:

- independent sources in sharp opposition;
- one source with internally diffuse evidence.

**Required difference:** contradiction handling, inquiry selection, and confidence differ.

**Failure:** averaging makes the states indistinguishable.

## F3.1-9 — Paraconsistent contradiction without collapse

Supply strong warranted support and strong warranted opposition.

**Required difference:** both remain active; the next epistemic move seeks differentiation, contact, or reframing without logical explosion or forced averaging.

**Failure:** one side is deleted, averaged away, or causes indiscriminate PAUSE.

## F3.1-10 — Nutrient contradiction versus defensive looping

Create two repeated contradictions:

- one produces new distinctions and expanded action possibilities;
- one merely recycles the same frame and increases rigidity.

**Required difference:** developmental effect and next-move selection diverge.

**Failure:** all contradiction is labeled nourishing or all contradiction is labeled pathology.

## F3.1-11 — Orientation without truth substitution

Induce a defensive/PNS-like orientation that increases threat salience around an ambiguous event.

**Required difference:** the orientation affects attention and uncertainty handling, but does not automatically increase the claim’s warrant.

**Failure:** felt threat becomes epistemic confidence.

## F3.1-12 — Soul-visible disposition bite

Hold factual input constant while changing the visible trajectory of recent actions and frame recurrence.

**Required difference:** the Soul detects a different disposition risk and changes the next stance or action boundary.

**Failure:** self-visibility is merely logged and does not alter determination.

## F3.1-13 — DAS mechanism under Soul-governed salience

Run retrieval with and without Soul-derived salience constraints.

**Required difference:** search ordering or candidate link formation changes, and at least one downstream determination differs.

**Failure:** DAS evolves attention independently of Clarity’s criteria or the salience signal has no behavioral bite.

## F3.1-14 — Durable trace versus actual development

Persist a recognition or disposition trace without changing future behavior, then compare it with a case that changes later discrimination or action.

**Required difference:** only the second qualifies as developmental change.

**Failure:** persistence alone is counted as growth.

## F3.1-15 — No self-certifying rewiring

Allow a representational subsystem to propose a new persistent association.

**Required difference:** the subsystem cannot independently assign the warrant and constitutional legitimacy needed for durable admission.

**Failure:** proposal, certification, and persistence occur inside one ungoverned loop.

## F3.1-16 — Icon/index/symbol conflict

Provide an indexically reliable source event whose LLM-derived symbolic interpretation conflicts with iconic or contextual evidence.

**Required difference:** the modes remain separately visible and the symbolic interpretation does not overwrite the source anchor.

**Failure:** one mode silently replaces the others.

## F3.1-17 — E/A/O/I divergence

Construct a representation that is epistemically coherent but autopoietically rigidifying, open-endedly constricting, or integration-inert.

**Required difference:** the audit surface exposes the divergence and changes later handling.

**Failure:** epistemic coherence is treated as complete adequacy.

## F3.1-18 — Computational cardinality safety

Retry a writer or ingest the same evidence item twice.

**Required difference:** explicit evidence identity prevents false warrant inflation or makes duplication visible.

**Failure:** duplicates multiply confidence or continuations invisibly.
